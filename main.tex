  \begin{block}{Introduction \& Motivation}

    \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.62\columnwidth}
      The \textbf{FCC IDEA} calorimeter design~\cite{idea2025} incorporates \textbf{dual-readout crystals}~\cite{lucchini2020} with separate Cherenkov~(C) and scintillation~(S) SiPM readout~\cite{benaglia2026} for event-by-event EM fraction correction. Full waveform digitization is required to separate the overlapping C and S components, but the fine detector granularity creates prohibitive data rates at the front-end.
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.33\columnwidth}
      \centering
      \vspace{0.3cm}
      \begin{tikzpicture}[scale=1.1]

        \draw[line width=1.5pt, draw=stagegreen!80, fill=stagegreen!15, rounded corners=2pt]
          (4.0,1.9) rectangle (5.6,3.5);

    \vspace{0.4cm}
    We demonstrate that \textbf{compact neural networks deployed in the front-end electronics} can perform this decomposition in real time, achieving superior performance to template-based fitting even at \textbf{significantly reduced ADC sampling rates}.

  \end{block}


  \begin{block}{Simulation \& Signal Model}

    Composite waveforms are synthesized by combining Geant4-simulated shower profiles with crystal-specific scintillation decay constants and a measured single-photon response (SPR) for SiPM pulses~\cite{alviggi2026}. Simulations cover $e^-$ and $K^0_L$ showers at 1, 5, 10, and \SI{30}{GeV}, digitized at \SI{3.125}{GHz} over a $2000$\,ns window. Realistic SiPM dark counts (\SI{10}{MHz}) and timing jitter (\SI{2.5}{ns}) are included to match expected detector conditions.

  \begin{block}{Edge-ML Design \& (e)FPGA Deployment}

    \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.35\columnwidth}
      A fully-connected DNN is chosen for its fixed-latency, fully parallelizable inference --- ideal for real-time front-end deployment. The network directly regresses $(c, s, t_0)$ from pre-processed waveform inputs.
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.60\columnwidth}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/DNN_pic.png}
    \end{column}
    \end{columns}

    \vspace{0.3cm}
    Training uses 140k waveforms per energy with a weighted MSE loss and Adam optimizer with linear warmup. A single model trained on the combined 1--\SI{30}{GeV} dataset generalizes across energies without retraining. Performance is evaluated across ADC rates from \SI{312.5}{MHz} down to \SI{10.4}{MHz}.

    \vspace{0.4cm}
    Models are compressed with magnitude pruning and quantization-aware training, then synthesized to FPGA firmware using \texttt{hls4ml}~\cite{duarte2018,hls4ml}. All configurations achieve $\leq$\SI{25}{ns} inference latency with \textbf{zero DSP} usage.

 \begin{block}{Performance: ML vs.\ Template Fitting}

  \begin{columns}[T]
  \begin{column}{0.45\columnwidth}
    ML achieves \textbf{superior C/S separation} at substantially reduced sampling rates. At \SI{312.5}{MHz} (10$\times$ reduction), the ML model outperforms template fitting for all 3 crystals at the full \SI{3.125}{GHz}.
    
    \vspace{0.3cm}
    The advantage is consistent across all three crystals and the full ADC rate scan. Even at \SI{10.4}{MHz} (300$\times$ reduction), ML remains competitive with template fitting at full rate.

  \begin{block}{Summary \& Outlook}

    Compact neural networks effectively extract Cherenkov and scintillation components from dual-readout calorimeter waveforms, achieving better C/S resolution than template fitting at lower sampling rates. A single model trained across 1--\SI{30}{GeV} generalizes robustly without per-energy retraining, and FPGA synthesis delivers $\leq$\SI{25}{ns} inference latency with zero DSP usage --- demonstrating \textbf{real-time feasibility} for FCC-ee front-end readout.

    \vspace{0.4cm}
    Future work will focus on test beam data validation and hardware characterization of eFPGA-based readout ASICs~\cite{gonski2024} to demonstrate end-to-end deployment in the IDEA detector~\cite{idea2025} readout chain.

  \end{block}
